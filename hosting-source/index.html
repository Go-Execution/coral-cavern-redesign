KNOW YOUR REEF

Find your coral.
Find your flow.

The best coral is not simply the brightest one. Match its light, flow, spacing and stability needs to the reef you already have.

LARGE POLYP STONY

LPS corals.

Large, expressive polyps bring movement to the middle and lower reef. Many LPS corals prefer moderate light, gentle indirect flow and enough room that expanding tissue or sweeper tentacles do not meet a neighbor.

Moderate light for many speciesSpace for full expansion
Find your LPS
SMALL POLYP STONY

SPS corals.

Fine polyps and architectural growth make SPS corals striking upper-reef choices. They usually reward brighter light, stronger variable flow and a mature system where alkalinity and nutrients stay consistent.

Bright, well-distributed lightStable mature systems

GOOD TO KNOW

Before you dive in.

Practical answers for choosing livestock, planning care and knowing what to expect before anything enters your water.

What does WYSIWYG mean?

“What you see is what you get.” A WYSIWYG listing shows the individual specimen offered. Confirm the current specimen and availability before ordering.

Which corals suit my first reef?

Start with a fully cycled, stable system. Mushroom and other soft corals can be useful starting points, but lighting, flow and tank mates still matter. We can help you compare suitable options.

Can I visit Coral Cavern?

Find us at 1341 Ranier Loop NW, Salem, Oregon. Call (971) 374-4572 to confirm a visit time before traveling.

How do shipping and livestock protection work?

Shipping method, dispatch timing and protection eligibility should be confirmed for your order. Report arrival concerns immediately, keep the packaging and document the animal. Read the current written terms before purchase.

Do you work with aquariums and ponds?

Request an estimate for aquarium design, installation, relocation or maintenance, and pond care. Share your location and setup so the team can confirm service coverage and scope.

Are the photographs actual products?

Livestock cards use product photographs from the supplied Coral Cavern reference catalogue. Stock photographs appear only in editorial/category sections. Where an actual specimen photo cannot be confirmed, we request a photo rather than substitute a different animal.

What should I share when asking for a recommendation?

Tank volume and age, recent salinity and temperature, lighting model and schedule, flow, current fish and corals, and a full-tank photo all help. The more context we have, the easier it is to recommend an animal that fits rather than one that only looks right.
